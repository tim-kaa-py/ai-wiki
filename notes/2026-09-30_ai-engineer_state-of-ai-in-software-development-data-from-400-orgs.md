# Ingest Notes

**Source:** [The State of AI in Software Development: Data from 400+ Orgs — Justin Reock, DX](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Se8jHLliLXE)

## User Focus

- What is the average productivity gain from AI?
- Why is it not higher?
- What are the trends?
- How are different productivity metrics affected by AI, and why?

## Confirmed Discoveries

- **A** [04:00–04:30] METR study: 16 devs ~19% slower but felt ~20% faster (~40-point perception gap); speaker calls it flawed and partly walked back. Contrasts with DX's flat aggregate perception.
- **B** [10:37–13:27] DX AI Measurement Framework: utilization → impact → cost as a maturity curve; compare AI-user cohorts against trusted foundational metrics (PR cycle time, PR size, review pushback).
- **D** [16:21–18:31] Case studies of attacking non-code bottlenecks (company-reported): Morgan Stanley DevGen AI (legacy code → PRDs, ~300k hours/yr), Zapier (~15% more value per engineer, hiring more, stand-ups 5→2/week, ~2-week onboarding, ~3k automated superficial reviews/week), Spotify SRE incident-context agent.

## Data Caveats (from Focus & Discovery)

- Vendor data (DX sells measurement tooling); case-study figures are company self-reports.
- Sample per figure unclear: 400+ orgs in title, ~200,000 engineers cited only at [06:17]; CFR chart is an unstated subset.
- Study window "November to 2024 to February this year" is garbled; treat as ambiguous.
- CFR "+2%" vs ~4% benchmark yields "~50% more defects" only if percentage points.
- Survey deltas (4.5%, 4%, −6%, −10%) unclear whether relative or absolute.
- 14–16% value-stream share of code generation is unsourced.
- Causal explanations (regions, company size, PR size, CFR) are the speaker's interpretation.
- Whisper transcript renders the speaker's name "Riach"; correct is Reock.
